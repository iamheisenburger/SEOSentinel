Free SEO content writing software can help you draft articles, organize topics, and move posts onto your site. It cannot decide what your business is or verify your claims for you. The practical question is which parts of the work a free tool covers, and which parts you still have to do yourself.

This article explains how to evaluate free options without relying on marketing claims, what a free tier typically has to be checked for, and where a paid or autopilot-style product fits. The guidance below is a set of checks you can run yourself and a proposed evaluation framework, not established industry fact.

## Start with the job, not the label

"SEO content writing software" covers several different jobs. Before comparing tools, separate them:

1. **Finding topics.** Deciding which questions your customers ask and which pages you should create.
2. **Drafting.** Turning a topic and a set of facts into a readable article.
3. **Checking.** Confirming that every claim in the draft is true for your business.
4. **Publishing.** Getting the article into your CMS or site build, with correct metadata.
5. **Measuring.** Seeing whether the page gets impressions and clicks, and what to change.

A tool that only drafts text covers step 2. A tool that also publishes covers step 4. In the author's view, the step most likely to be skipped is step 3, so it is worth checking whether a free tool includes a fact review at all. If you skip it, you may publish confident-sounding claims about prices, hours, certifications, or service areas that are wrong.

## What to check in any free plan

Free plans can change, so verify the following on the vendor's current page before you sign up. Write down the answers in a simple table:

- **Monthly or word limit.** Is it per article, per month, or per workspace? Compare the limit against how many articles you actually plan to publish.
- **Credit card requirement.** Does the plan need payment details? This matters for testing on a real site.
- **Business facts input.** Does the tool ask you for your services, locations, and offers, or does it only take a keyword? Articles written from keywords alone are the most likely to contain invented details.
- **Fact review.** Is there a step that checks claims against the facts you supplied, or do you have to do it all manually?
- **Publishing targets.** Does it publish to your platform directly, or do you copy and paste? Copy-and-paste adds time but gives you a manual review point.
- **Search data.** Does it connect to Google Search Console so you can see clicks and positions after publishing?
- **Export and ownership.** Can you export your drafts and published content if you leave?

Use this as a scoring sheet. A tool that scores well on drafting but has no fact review step should be treated as a drafting aid, not a publishing system.

## A reader-run test before you commit

Instead of trusting a feature list, run a short test with a real page on your own site:

1. Pick one service page you already know well, with accurate prices and details.
2. Give the tool only the facts you would confirm for a new visitor. Keep a copy of the facts.
3. Generate one article on a topic close to that service.
4. Compare every sentence containing a number, date, price, location, or claim against your facts list.
5. Count how many corrections you had to make, and note how long the review took.

**Observable result:** You will know whether the free tier saves time after review, or whether review eats the time saved. If you find an unsupported claim, the tool is not safe to run unattended, regardless of how polished the draft reads.

This test is a diagnostic you run yourself. Its result depends on your site, your facts, and the tool you pick.

## Why keyword research and fact review matter more than word count

Free drafting tools often focus on length and structure. For SEO, the bigger issues are usually:

- **Topic fit.** Does the article answer a question your customers actually ask, or a generic one? Check the queries the page receives in Google Search Console once it is live, and compare them against the intent of the page.
- **Accuracy.** A wrong price or a made-up credential damages trust with readers who may become customers.
- **Internal consistency.** If your service page gives one opening time and the article gives another, both pages lose credibility.

Keep a simple fact sheet for your business. It should include services, service areas, prices or price ranges you are willing to publish, hours, credentials you can document, and the booking or signup page. Every article should draw from that sheet. If an article needs a fact that is not on the sheet, add it to the sheet first, or remove the claim.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is free SEO content writing software enough for a small business?**
It can be, if the free tier covers your publishing pace and you review every article against your facts. If the free limit is smaller than the number of pages you need, you will either pay or write the rest yourself.

**Do I still need to edit AI-drafted articles?**
Yes. Treat any draft as unverified until you have checked its facts, prices, dates, and claims. The review step is where most of the risk is controlled.

**What should I measure after publishing?**
Use Google Search Console to see impressions, clicks, and average position for the page's queries. Compare these before and after publishing. Decide in advance how long you will watch a page before judging it, since a short window of data can be misleading.
